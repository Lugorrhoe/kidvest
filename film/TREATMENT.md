# Kidvest film: creative treatment

Working title: **"2008, for a seven-year-old"**. 90 seconds, 1920 x 1080, 30 fps, silent-safe.

## The story in one breath

How do you explain 2008 to a seven-year-old? You don't. You let her live through it, with ten coins at stake instead of ten thousand, and then you talk about it. Kidvest is the whole arc: it **shows** a century of real markets in disguise, it **lets** a child **do** the one thing that matters in investing (hold on when it storms), and it hands the parents the real story afterwards so they can **talk**.

## The red thread: one year, three views

The entire film is built around a single real year, 2008, seen three times:

1. **SHOW**: 2008 as a dip on a 97-year chart, then as an honest but vague forecast on a sunny farm ("Farmer Jo warns: this could be a very stormy year").
2. **LET DO**: 2008 as weather. Rain, then storm, red chips on the cows, a child who presses "Next season" and holds. Then spring 2009, green chips, a badge, a real reward.
3. **TALK**: 2008 by its name, for the parents only. The Reports timeline reveals "Year 6 was 2008, the global financial crisis. Your child held on through the storm," with a conversation starter.

Everything the audience sees is the real product rendering real engine states. No invented interactions. The only deliberate action the child takes is to keep going: that is the educational thesis (patience over trading, behaviour over luck), so the film does not glamorise buying and selling.

## Visual metaphor

**Weather.** The product already translates markets into weather for the youngest bracket; the film adopts it as its language. The hook's data line becomes the farm's horizon (match cut). The LET DO chapter darkens the sky and clears it again. The TALK chapter sets the stormy farm (child's view) beside the "2008" timeline row (parent's view) on the same horizontal line: same moment, two truths.

## Design language

- **Stage**: the product's ink-900 (#14171d). Product UI floats as a device-less card with a soft, deep shadow, lit by a faint radial accent per world (farm green, space violet, city teal). No glow effects, no particles, no gratuitous 3D.
- **Typography**: Inter (the product's declared typeface), tabular numerals. Captions 56 to 72 px, weight 700, tracking -0.02 em, white, one line, never more than seven words. A persistent chapter label (SHOW / LET DO / TALK) in 18 px spaced capitals, top left, 60 % white. Kidvest wordmark bottom left at 40 % white from the first farm shot on.
- **Motion**: one easing family. Enters: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.1, 1), 0.6 to 0.9 s. Camera: ease in and out, 1.5 to 5 s, never faster than the eye reads. No bounce, no overshoot. The cursor is a soft white dot with a click ring, not an arrow.
- **Four signature transitions**, each with a narrative job:
  - T1 *Line to horizon*: the real S&P line tweens into the farm's hill curve; colour floods the frame. "History becomes a place."
  - T2 *Age rail*: a horizontal dolly with parallax across farm, starbase and city. "Same data, three ages." Reverses without a cut.
  - T3 *Two views*: the stormy farm docks left while the parent timeline slides in right, the 2008 row aligned with the storm. "What she felt, what it was."
  - T4 *Pull back*: all three worlds recede in a shallow 3D fan under the three chapter words, then the logo. "One tool, the whole arc."

## Emotional arc

Curiosity (a hard question), warmth (a sunny farm, a child's world), unease (the forecast, the darkening sky), tension (storm, "Hold."), relief and pride (spring, badge, ice cream), recognition (parents see 2008 by name), resolve (the lockup, the logo).

## Sound

The film must work silently, and in the pitch Lukas may speak over it. Default plan: a minimal, procedurally generated piano and pad bed at about 68 BPM (legally clean, generated by a script in this repo), with four soft UI clicks, a low rumble under the storm, a short chime at the badge and a whoosh at T1 and T3. A licensed track can be dropped into `film/public/music.mp3` to replace the generated bed without touching the timeline. No voice-over: the captions carry the narrative.
