# Kidvest

**Children learn to invest by doing it, one season at a time.**

Kidvest is a browser game for children aged 6 to 14 and their parents. A century of real market history (1928 to 2025) is replayed in disguise: as a farm, a space station or a city. Children buy, hold and sell, live through storms and booms, collect payouts, chase a savings goal and earn badges. The older the child, the closer the game gets to a real trading app.

Built for the TUM seminar *Smart Start: Financial, Tax, and Legal Fundamentals for Entrepreneurs* (winter term 2026/27), topic T12: *What is the best way for children to learn how to handle money?* The design follows the pitch's thesis: **show, let do, then talk.** Kidvest is the "let do" part; the parent reports provide the "then talk" part.

> Educational simulation. No real money, no accounts, no tracking. Not financial advice.

## Three age groups, three worlds

| Age group | Default world | What the child sees |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 8 · Explorer | 🌻 Sunny Hill Farm | Whole animals and trees, coins, weather instead of risk numbers, a yearly harvest, no percentages |
| 9 to 11 · Builder | 🪐 Starbase Nova | Space modules, percent changes, charts, running costs, payout vs. reinvest, a cheap vs. expensive twin |
| 12 to 14 · Investor | 🏙️ Metro City | Real terms (ETF, bonds, TER, small caps, gold, real estate), euros, order fees, inflation-adjusted view |

Any world can be combined with any age group, so siblings can play the same world at different depths.

## One year a day

The default rhythm is about ten minutes a day. When the child opens Kidvest, a **year report** appears: Farmer Jo's almanac, a mission-control briefing or an economic outlook, written for the age group, hinting at how the coming year may go ("Farmer Jo smells storms coming"). Then the child plays the year's **four seasons**, deciding before each one. After the fourth season a **year review** shows what happened per asset, checks the forecast, and the world rests until the unlock time the next day (default 10:00, set by the parent).

The forecasts are built from the real year being replayed. For 6 to 8 they are always right but vague. For 9 to 11 one "rumour" per year may be wrong. For 12 to 14 the "analyst consensus" is wrong about a quarter of the time, and the review says so: predictions are one input, patience and diversification matter more. Parents can also choose free play (unlimited seasons) or real-time mode (seasons tick by themselves).

## The monthly money talk

The educational value lives in the conversation, so Kidvest nags the parents, gently. In *Parent area → Reports* parents set a day and time for a monthly talk, download a **recurring calendar reminder** (.ics with a pop-up and an e-mail alert; works with Outlook, Google and Apple Calendar), or open a prefilled e-mail to themselves. When a month has passed since the last talk, a banner appears on the start page and in the parent area. A **talk sheet** collects the conversation prompts per child and a five-step agenda; "We talked today" resets the timer.

Kidvest has no server and therefore cannot send e-mails by itself; the calendar reminder is the reliable path.

## A world that keeps living

Kidvest is meant to be checked from time to time, like a real portfolio, not binge-played.

* **Real-time mode (optional).** Seasons tick by themselves while the app is closed (per minute, hour, day, 3 days or week); on the next visit a "Welcome back" summary shows what happened.
* **Endless chapters.** When a scenario's history window ends, a new chapter starts automatically and history continues (after 2025 it jumps to a seeded random earlier year). Prices, holdings and progress carry over.
* **A goal ladder.** Children save up for a list of things (farm dog, red tractor, sunflower field, big farmhouse ...; robot helper, explorer ship, telescope ...; e-scooter, laptop, festival trip, gap year, first car). Prices rise with inflation. Buying one needs cash, so the child has to decide what to sell. Bought items stay in the world as decorations.

## Real-life rewards (parents)

Parents can promise something real for something the child does in the game: a money bonus, extra pocket money, a gift, an outing or a privilege. Kidvest tracks the condition and tells both sides when it is earned; the parent marks it as handed over.

* **Conditions** favour behaviour over luck: no panic selling for N seasons, keeping at least X % invested, owning several kinds, saving up and buying a goal item, earning badges, completing challenges, checking in on N days. Outcome conditions (grow by X %, reach a value, beat inflation) exist for older children and longer periods.
* **Timed or untimed.** Untimed rewards wait until earned (recommended for the youngest). Timed rewards run 2 weeks to 6 months in real time; missed deadlines are shown to the child with a gentle note and can be offered again.
* **Suggestions per age group** prefill the form, and a short research-based guide (reward behaviour not luck, small and soon for young children, experiences over cash, keep it predictable) sits next to it.

## The world view

On the farm each asset has its own place: cows in a red barn, hens in a coop, pigs in a mud enclosure, bees in hives, apple trees in an orchard. Buildings grow through **four build-out stages** as the child owns more (a shed becomes a barn, gets a silo, then a second barn), the animals move in a fitting way (cows graze, hens peck, pigs wiggle, bees buzz, trees sway), and **hovering** any place shows its stats: how many, worth, what was paid, change since purchase and since the last visit, what it gives, and what it takes to reach the next stage.

Above the portfolio, every child sees their world, in the spirit of farming games: the coin barn / starbase core / cash account holding the cash, one pen (field, dock or city block) per asset with as many animals, modules or buildings as the child owns, a value sign, and a chip with **what this asset gained or lost since the last season or the last visit**. Unowned assets appear faded with a price tag. The sky reflects the market: sun, clouds, rain or a storm on the farm and in the city, solar wind or a meteor shower in space.

## Features

**For children:** trading-app style dashboard with total value, available cash and invested amount; growth chart with time ranges (1, 3, 5 years, all) and two comparison lines (what the same money would be if only saved as cash, or kept in a savings account at that year's interest rate, both net of goal purchases); allocation donut and risk indicator; market with sparklines; buy/sell ticket adapted to the age group; seasonal recap with payouts, pocket money, storms and story news; an inflation-linked savings goal; scenario challenges; 13 badges; five levels per world; end-of-round summary for scenarios that are set to end.

**Gamification that rewards patience, not activity.** Badges are earned for holding through a storm, diversifying, keeping costs low, reaching a goal and having "steady hands", never for trading often. There are no streaks, loot boxes or notifications.

**For parents (PIN protected):** manage children (age group, world, scenario); create and edit scenarios (history window, start money, pocket money, order fee, assets, goal, story events with optional price shocks, challenges); create custom assets that follow a real series (with a multiplier) or a custom random model; reports with the real years replayed, orders and **conversation prompts** generated after storms, panic sales, milestones and risky bets; JSON backup, import and reset.

## How the market works

* Annual returns 1928 to 2025 for the S&P 500 (incl. dividends), US small caps (bottom decile), 3-month T-bills, 10-year Treasuries, Baa corporate bonds, US house prices and gold come from Aswath Damodaran, *Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills* (NYU Stern, updated January 2026). Inflation is the US CPI-U, December over December (Bureau of Labor Statistics).
* Each year is split into four seasons. The four seasons always compound **exactly** to the official annual figure. The shape inside a year is approximated: known quarterly patterns are used for famous years (1987, 2000 to 2002, 2008, 2009, 2020, 2022), all other years get a seeded random shape, so every run is reproducible.
* Distributing assets split the total return into a price change and a cash payout; accumulating assets keep everything in the price. Running costs (TER) are deducted every season; order fees are charged per trade.
* Goals rise with inflation; age group 3 can switch the chart to "after inflation".

See `src/data/history.ts` and `src/engine/market.ts`. Unit tests (`npm test`) check that seasons compound to the annual data and that payouts plus price equal the total return.

## Try it without installing anything

`npm run build:single` produces one self-contained file, `dist-single/index.html`. Double-click it and it runs in Chrome, Edge or Firefox, fully offline. A prebuilt copy is shipped next to this folder as `Kidvest.html`.

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 20 or newer.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test
npm run build      # static site in dist/
```

Everything is stored in the browser's IndexedDB (via Dexie). There is no server and no account. Use *Parent area → Data & settings* to export a backup.

## Publish on GitHub Pages

1. Create a GitHub repository and push this folder to the `main` branch.
2. In the repository go to *Settings → Pages* and set *Source* to **GitHub Actions**.
3. The workflow in `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` tests, builds and deploys on every push. The app uses relative paths and hash routing, so it works under any repository name, e.g. `https://<user>.github.io/kidvest/`.
