Follows slide 9 ("Show, let do, then talk") and the transition slide 10. Target 2:00 at about 150 words per minute, roughly 300 spoken words. The video runs without sound; you narrate live. Structure: start page (what and how), the three worlds by hovering only, the farm in depth, then the parent area for the last 45 seconds.

## Preparation before recording

1. Load the demo family ("Try the demo family"), so the worlds are mid-game and Thanja's next year is 2008.
2. Parent area → Children → Thanja → Edit: set the rhythm to "Free play", so the "Next season" button is available while recording.
3. Parent area → Rewards → Thanja: add "Ice cream outing" from the suggestions (condition "own several kinds", untimed). She already owns four kinds, so it is earned the moment her farm opens.
4. Keep the parent area unlocked in a second tab for the last scene.
5. Record in Chrome at 1440 px or wider. Rest the mouse for a second on anything you hover, so the hover cards are visible in the recording.

## Spoken text only (about 300 words, for rehearsal)

My pitch ended with "show, let do, then talk". Kidvest is the "let do" part. It is an investing game for children from six to fourteen. I built it with Claude, it runs in the browser without any server, and every price in it is real: it replays almost a century of market history, in disguise.

One engine, three worlds. Six to eight year olds run a farm, nine to eleven a space station, twelve to fourteen a city where the real words appear: ETF, bonds, inflation. Same data underneath. Today I show you the farm.

Thanja is seven. Her cows are a dividend ETF, the pig is a small-cap stock, the hens are bonds. She does not know that. She knows that Farmer Jo expects storms, because the year she is about to play is 2008. She plays one year a day: four seasons, about ten minutes, then the farm rests.

Here is the storm. Her cows lose value, and the chart shows what the same pocket money would have done in a jar or in a savings account. A seven-year-old learns what a crash feels like, with ten coins at stake instead of ten thousand.

The year is over and the farm rests until tomorrow. She did not sell in the storm, and because she spread her coins over four kinds of animals she has earned something real. Parents set these rewards for behaviour, never for luck.

And this is the "then talk" part, for parents. They set the rhythm. They see which real years their child just lived through. After every storm and every sale they get a conversation prompt, and once a month a reminder to actually sit down and talk, because that is where the learning sticks. Show, let do, then talk. That is Kidvest. Thank you.

## Q&A fallbacks

- Data: annual returns 1928 to 2025 from Damodaran (NYU Stern), US CPI from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Each year is split into four seasons that compound exactly to the real annual figure.
- "Where is the AI?" The tool contains no AI model by design; it was built with Claude, which matches the kick-off wording "build with AI, e.g. Lovable". Children's data never leaves the browser.
- Forecasts: for the youngest they are always right but vague; for the oldest the "analyst consensus" is deliberately wrong a quarter of the time and the year review says so.
- Rewards: conditions favour behaviour (holding through storms, diversifying, saving for a goal) over outcomes, because returns are mostly the market's doing.
